Forecasts How the school and dwelling-value forecasts are made and tested

An exercise, not advice. These forecasts are an experiment in how far public data can be pushed, published with their errors. They are not predictions to rely on, and not a guide to choosing a school or a home.

What is forecast

  • Schools. Each English public or Catholic elementary school's Grade 3 and Grade 6 score, one test year ahead. A score is the mean of the school's reading, writing and math results: the share of its fully participating students at or above the provincial standard. A neighbourhood's figure is its schools' forecasts weighted by enrolment. Grade 3 and Grade 6 are forecast separately.
  • Dwelling value. Each of the 158 neighbourhoods' census median value of owned dwellings, as a ratio to the City's median, one census (five years) ahead. The City's own median is not forecast; for dollar figures it is nowcast from TRREB's sale prices (below).

Every model places each school or neighbourhood against the City (a school by its score on the logit scale less the City's enrolment-weighted mean), and the City's level carries over from the year the forecast is made, the origin. The models differ only in where they place each unit.

The models

The models are rungs on a ladder, from the simplest up. Each is tried in every backtest; the decision rule below picks one.

Predictors: only what was known at the origin

  • Schools. The school's position at the origin (also squared, and scaled by its enrolment), the year before and its mean to date; the other grade's position; enrolment; Catholic or public; the Ministry's six student shares (low income, parents without a degree, first language not English, new to Canada, special education, gifted), standardised within each year; and the neighbourhood's census (median after-tax household income, low-income rate, degrees, renters, recent immigrants, movers, children). A census is used from two test years after it: 2016 for the origins 2018 to 2022, 2021 from 2023.
  • Dwelling value. The census at the origin (income, low income, renters, condominiums, detached houses, tall apartments, new dwellings, degrees, recent immigrants, movers, children, seniors, shelter costs), the five-year change in seven of them, the neighbouring neighbourhoods' mean ratio, and permits issued in the five years before per 1,000 households. The variants add age and arrival shares and the change in owners; dwelling shares; the previous five-year change in the ratio of average values; and heavy building.

The backtests

Each backtest fits the models on what came before an origin and forecasts what followed. Years are test years (2025 is the 2024–25 school year). Scores to 2025 are the Ministry's; 2026 scores are from EQAO's school file, which reproduces every Ministry 2024–25 Toronto score.

Scores and the decision rule

  • Error. The mean absolute error: in percentage points for schools, weighted by enrolment; for dwelling value, of the log ratio to the City's median (×100, about a percentage), weighted by households. Skill is the reduction in error against persistence.
  • The rule. Walk up the ladder from persistence. Up to ridge, a rung replaces the one chosen so far only if its error is lower in every fold. Past ridge, its mean improvement must also be larger than the spread of its improvements across the folds. For dwelling value the folds are forward and reverse, where a rung has both.
  • Centring (dwelling value, adopted 1 November 2026 after looking). Each rung's predicted changes are centred to average zero over households, so no rung carries one period's average shift against the City's median into the next. Raw scores are shown alongside.

The rule chose ridge regression for schools and own mean for dwelling value.

The ranges

  • Schools. An 80% range: the forecast plus or minus the 80th percentile of ridge's absolute neighbourhood errors in the two selection folds, set separately for neighbourhoods with one school and with more. Fixed before the holdout; the holdout and 2025 to 2026 report how many neighbourhoods fell inside.
  • Dwelling value. From the lowest of three forecasts less 0.05 on the log scale, to the highest plus 0.05. The three are persistence (the neighbourhood's standing against the City's median at the last census), own mean (its average standing over the last three censuses) and own mean plus market (own mean, raised or lowered by how the neighbourhood's sale prices have moved against the City's since that census). The 0.05 allows for the neighbourhoods' average shift against the City's median and the error in the City's nowcast. Set after the backtest's point scores were seen; its coverage on 2016 to 2021 was scored once afterwards and the rule left unchanged.

The live forecasts

  • Schools. Ridge refitted on every one-year transition to 2026 forecasts 2027 (the 2026–27 school year), from EQAO's 2026 scores. For 2026, enrolment and the student shares are the Ministry's 2024–25 figures, the latest it publishes. The forecast for 2026, made from 2025 and committed before any 2025–26 result was read, is scored above as 2025 to 2026.
  • Dwelling value. The 2026 Census (reference day May 2026; Statistics Canada plans its major releases by December 2027). The City's median is its 2021 Census median moved by TRREB's May median sale price, 2021 to 2026, with nothing fitted. That rule was scored on 2016 to 2021 against the Census.
