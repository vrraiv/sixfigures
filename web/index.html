Toronto, mapped by its data

Six Figures gathers the open data published about the City of Toronto: the census, school results, home sales, construction and rents, crime and road safety, trees and summer heat. It puts all of it on the same map of the city's 158 neighbourhoods, so that anything can be looked up, ranked and compared with anything else.

Three ways in

  1. Start with a place

    Type an address above to see its own few blocks on the census map. To see a whole neighbourhood against the rest of the city, open the Neighbourhood Explorer; if you don't know the City's name for yours, its address box will find it.

  2. Start with a subject

    Each of the Topics is one page about one subject, such as schools or safety: the city's figures first, then maps, rankings and trends for the neighbourhoods.

  3. Start with a question

    Do higher incomes go with better school results? Which areas have the least shade? The Data Explorer sets any two or three measures side by side, from anywhere on the site.

The menu at the top of every page has the same links as the cards below, under the same names.

Good to know

  • Two sizes of area. Most pages show the City's 158 neighbourhoods. The census maps go finer, to dissemination areas: the census's smallest areas, a few blocks and a few hundred people each.
  • Everything responds. Point at (or tap) a map or chart to read its figures, and select an area for more about it. Menus above a map or chart change what it shows.
  • Darker means more. Maps shade from light to dark as the number rises. Grey means there is no figure for that area.
  • Sources are at the foot of each page, and the About page lists them all, with what to keep in mind when reading them.
